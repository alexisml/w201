# Suspension: refresh and sport upgrade

**System:** suspension
**Status:** planned

Plan for refreshing the suspension and going to a sport setup (lowering springs such as H&R or
Eibach, with Bilstein dampers). Mercedes part numbers come from the EPC; spring part numbers come
from retailer listings; brand and upgrade advice is general W201 knowledge and owner reports,
marked with its confidence. Apart from the bushings, nothing here has been checked on the car yet.

## What the car has

- The [data card](../general-reference/data-card.md) lists no suspension option codes, so the car
  should have the standard suspension (no Sportline, no rear self-levelling). *Not yet checked on
  the car: brand and age of the fitted dampers, and the spring pads (count the burls).*
- How the front works: the W201 front is not a coilover. The spring sits on the lower control arm
  on its own, and the damper is separate, held at the top by a rubber mount in the inner wing (3
  studs). The rear is multi-link, with the spring and the shock also separate.
- Bushings: all replaced about a year ago (around October 2025), with an alignment at the same time.

## Top mounts: which brand

*General knowledge, medium confidence. No sources saved yet.*

| Brand | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Genuine Mercedes | Best. Often only a little more than aftermarket for these parts, so compare prices first |
| Lemförder / Boge (both ZF) | Original suppliers to Mercedes. Safe choice |
| Corteco (Freudenberg) | Avoid for top mounts. Freudenberg supplies rubber parts to Mercedes, but buyer reviews of Corteco W201 top mounts are bad (seen by us 2026-10-11, no source saved). Today's aftermarket part may not be the part Mercedes got |
| Febi | Rebrands parts from different makers, so quality varies. Usually fine for mounts; the rubber may wear sooner |
| Unbranded, very cheap | Avoid. The rubber goes hard and cracks, the bond fails and the clunks come back. The work costs more than the part |

Plan: Lemförder, or genuine if the price is close. Read buyer reviews for the exact part number
before ordering, whatever the brand. Replace the top mount, the mounting parts kit,
bump stop, boot and self-locking nuts together, and do both sides.

## Sport upgrade: springs and dampers

Goal: a sportier look and handling, **not** a big drop. Target about 25–30 mm lower. Stock springs
are out; the choice is between H&R and Eibach.

### Springs

*Part numbers and drops from retailer listings seen 2026-10-11 (no links saved yet). Confirm on the
maker's own fitment page before buying, and save that page as the source.*

| Brand | Part number | Drop (front / rear) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H&R** sport spring | `29644-1` (US listings: `29644`) | about 33 / 32 mm (US listing, 1.3" / 1.25") | For the 190E 2.3 **without** self-levelling. Progressive. Closest to the target drop |
| **Eibach** Pro-Kit | `E2502-140` (EAN 4050278010504) | 40 / 30–40 mm | Listed for the 190E 2.3 (1986–91). Not for Sportline or self-levelling cars. Axle load limits 880 kg front, 940 kg rear |

Wrong numbers to avoid: H&R `29614-1` is for cars with self-levelling. H&R `29539-1` (40 mm) and
`29538-1` (45 mm) are other W201 variants; check fitment before considering them. Others seen
and ruled out: Vogtland (45/25 mm, nose-down rake), AST and Direnza (less known or budget), and
Mercedes Sportline (about 22 mm; sold mostly as a full kit with dampers and control arms, about
€900).

EU price for either set was about €210–235 (Portugal, VAT included). No seller found in Brazil.

### Dampers

- **Bilstein B6 Sport** (plan): firmer, standard length, works with a drop of about 30–40 mm.
  Bilstein has said B6 is fine with 40 mm H&R springs on other cars; no W201-specific report found.
- Bilstein B4: standard replacement, too soft for this goal.
- Bilstein B8: shortened body, only needed for bigger drops.
- Bilstein B12 Pro-Kit: Eibach springs and B8 dampers as one kit, more drop than wanted.

### Spring pads

The pads come in 5 mm steps (front 8 / 13 / 18 / 23 mm, rear 8 / 13 / 18 mm). Owners of the W124,
which uses the same pads, report each step changes the height at the arch by about 7–8 mm.

Plan: fit new pads with the springs, **one step thicker** than the spring maker asks for, which is
likely 2 burls (13 mm) front and rear instead of 1. That trims about 8–10 mm off the drop, so a
40 mm spring ends up at about 30 mm. After the car settles, adjust each corner by one step if it
sits unevenly. US-spec cars are heavier at the front, so the front may sit lower than listed.

### Bump stops

At 25–30 mm lower the stock bump stops should not need cutting. Fit new stock ones with new boots
(front bump stop `A 129 323 07 44`, boot `A 124 323 01 92`), unless the dampers come with their
own. Check after fitting: with the car at rest, if the gap from bump stop to damper body is under
about 30 mm, trim about 10 mm off. (The 30 mm gap is a rule of thumb, not a sourced figure.)

### Also

- **Alignment:** get it checked after the swap. Lowering adds rear negative camber, which can't be
  adjusted on the W201; front caster and toe, and rear toe, can.
- **Wheels and tyres:** lower plus wider wheels means checking clearance at the arches. See
  [Wheels and tyres](../wheels-tyres/wheels-and-tyres.md).
