# Wheels and tyres

**System:** wheels / tyres
**Status:** reference

Stock wheel and tyre sizes for this car, what to buy when the tyres are due, and the options for
going wider.

**Wheel bolts differ between wheels.** The thread length is the same for all Mercedes 15-hole
wheels, but the length of the bolt head varies with the wheel's offset. Aftermarket alloys and steel
wheels need their own bolts (Greg in Oz on
[PeachParts](https://www.peachparts.com/shopforum/tech-help/65636-1990-300e-wheel-size-dilemma.html),
[reference](references/peachparts-15-hole-wheel-offsets/README.md)). Check thread engagement whenever
the wheels change.

## Replacing the tyres (stock size)

- **185/65 R15** with load index **87 or higher** and speed rating **H or higher** (T is fine for
  winter tyres). 185/65 R15 is still a common size, so there is plenty of choice in touring tyres.
- Read the DOT date code (the last 4 digits are week and year). Old tyres harden and lose grip long
  before the tread wears out. Replace any that are much past 6 years old, whatever the tread depth.
- Fit the newest pair at the rear (it's rear-wheel drive and the rear losing grip is the worse case).

## Going wider

### How much each option moves the wheel

Starting from the stock 6 J ET 49. A wider rim grows by half its extra width on each side, and a
lower ET pushes the whole wheel outward.

| Wheel | Outer edge moves | Inner edge moves |
|---|---|---|
| 6.5 J × 15 ET 49 (W124) | 6 mm out | 6 mm in |
| 7 J × 15 ET 44 (Sportline / 2.3-16) | 18 mm out | 8 mm in |
| 8 J × 16 ET 34 (Evolution I) | 40 mm out | 10 mm in |

### Rolling diameter and speedometer

Stock 185/65 R15 is about **622 mm**. A smaller tyre makes the speedometer read higher than the real
speed (safe side); a bigger tyre makes it read lower.

| Tyre | Diameter | vs stock |
|---|---|---|
| 185/65 R15 (stock) | 622 mm | — |
| 195/65 R15 | 635 mm | +2.1 % |
| 205/60 R15 | 627 mm | +0.9 % |
| 195/60 R15 | 615 mm | −1.0 % |
| 205/55 R15 | 607 mm | −2.4 % |
| 205/50 R16 | 611 mm | −1.6 % |
| 205/55 R16 | 632 mm | +1.7 % |
| 225/45 R16 | 609 mm | −2.0 % |
| 225/50 R16 | 631 mm | +1.6 % |

### Options, from easiest to hardest

1. **195/65 R15 on the stock wheels.** Slightly wider and taller. The rim width suits it, but no
   source here lists it as approved for the W201, and it's 2 % taller. *General info, not verified on
   a W201.*
2. **Sportline / 2.3-16 wheels: 7 J × 15 ET 44 with 205/55 R15.** The factory's own wide option, and
   the best bet. Same 15-hole look, part `A 201 400 13 02`, made by Fuchs
   ([classic-parts-finder](https://classic-parts-finder.com/en/car-parts/mercedes-benz/190-w201/chassis/original-sportline-gullideckel-alloy-wheels-7jx15-et44_7306),
   [reference](references/cpf-sportline-gullideckel-7x15-et44/README.md);
   [mb190e16v.com](https://mb190e16v.com/technics/),
   [reference](references/mb190e16v-technics/README.md)). 205/55 R15 on 7 × 15 is listed for the
   190 E 2.3 (201.028) ([Autodoc](https://club.autodoc.co.uk/tyres/mercedes-benz/190/190-w201),
   [reference](references/autodoc-w201-approved-sizes/README.md)). A 190 E 2.6 owner swapped the
   regular 6.5 J wheels for the Sportline wheels and tyre size, and a Sportline owner was told
   205/55 R15 on W124 6.5 J wheels would make no real difference
   ([PeachParts](http://www.peachparts.com/shopforum/mercedes-benz-wheels-tires/31021-15x7-15x6-5-rims-190e-sportline-does-really-matter.html),
   [reference](../aesthetics/references/peachparts-sportline-rims/README.md)). Sportline cars were
   also lowered and had stiffer springs (see [sacco-cladding.md](../aesthetics/sacco-cladding.md));
   the wheels fit the standard height too, but they won't fill the arches the same way.
3. **W124 wheels: 6.5 J × 15 ET 49 (or 48).** Same look, 6 mm wider each side
   ([reference](references/peachparts-15-hole-wheel-offsets/README.md)). A 190 E 2.6 owner ran them
   with **205/60 R15** and it touched the front of the front wheel arches: they had to shave the lip
   slightly. With **205/55 R15** clearance was fine. They expect a 7 J aftermarket wheel to be a
   problem unless the ET is close to stock (Duke2.6 on
   [PeachParts](https://www.peachparts.com/shopforum/tech-help/422386-190d-2-5-wheels-tires.html),
   [reference](references/peachparts-190e-205-60-r15/README.md)). Note: the *Sportline* W124 wheel
   is 7 J ET 41, which sits further out than the 190's own 7 J ET 44.
4. **16-inch wheels, 7 J, with 205/50 R16.** One owner ran 205/50 R16 on a regular 190 E without
   rubbing; 205/55 R16 rubbed
   ([PeachParts](https://peachparts.com/shopforum/mercedes-benz-wheels-tires/78425-190e-16v-16-inch-rims-need-tire-advise.html),
   [reference](references/peachparts-190e-16v-16-inch-tyres/README.md)). Keep the ET in the 40s.
   *Owner reports, few data points.*
5. **Evolution I wheels: 8 J × 16 ET 34, 225/50 R16 (factory) or 225/45 R16.** Part
   `A 129 400 01 02`, originally an R129 SL wheel, also on the W124 E 500
   ([mb190e16v.com](https://mb190e16v.com/technics/),
   [reference](references/mb190e16v-technics/README.md)). Too wide for a normal 190 E, right for the
   16V with its flared arches. Even on a 2.3-16, 225/45 R16 is the best size and rubs lightly at
   the front edge of the front arch in hard corners; 225/50 R16 rubs badly
   ([PeachParts](https://peachparts.com/shopforum/mercedes-benz-wheels-tires/78425-190e-16v-16-inch-rims-need-tire-advise.html),
   [reference](references/peachparts-190e-16v-16-inch-tyres/README.md)). Would need arch work or
   16V / Evo flares on this car. Not recommended.

### What limits it

- **Front:** the front edge of the front arch, where the arch lip meets the bumper. That's where the
  205/60 R15 and the 225/50 R16 touched.
- **Rear:** the arch lip on full compression. Wider wheels with a lower ET usually need the lips
  rolled.
- Facelift **Sacco cladding** changes the arch edges a little: the European catalog has wider rear
  arch pieces "only with wide tyres" (see [sacco-cladding.md](../aesthetics/sacco-cladding.md)).
  Check clearance again after fitting the cladding.
- **Road legality** depends on where the car is registered. Some countries need the wheel/tyre
  combination approved or entered in the papers. Check before buying.

## Recommendation

- **Keep it simple:** good 185/65 R15 tyres, H rated, on the stock wheels.
- **Wider, still factory:** a set of Sportline / 2.3-16 wheels, 7 J × 15 ET 44, with 205/55 R15.
  Bolt-on, factory look, speedometer reads 2.4 % high. Check the bolts and the front arch clearance
  on full lock.
- Avoid 8 J wheels or low ETs on this car unless the arches are being changed.
